Why slow replies cost you jobs, and how to answer every inquiry in minutes

By Ryan Smith · November 11, 2026 · 6 min read

If you run a venue, a contracting business, a catering kitchen or anything that starts with someone filling out a form, here's an uncomfortable truth: the business that answers first usually gets the first real conversation, and often the job.

That's not just a hunch. In 2011, researchers writing in Harvard Business Review submitted test inquiries to 2,241 U.S. companies and timed the responses. Only 37% replied within an hour. The average response, among companies that replied at all, took 42 hours, and 23% never replied. The same research found that companies that reached out within an hour were nearly seven times more likely to have a meaningful conversation with the lead than companies that waited even an hour longer.

The study is old and it looked at larger companies, but the pattern still matches what I see with small local businesses. People send the same inquiry to three or four places. Whoever replies first, with a useful answer, sets the terms.

Why small businesses reply slowly

It's rarely laziness. It's usually one of these:

  • Inquiries come in from everywhere. A contact form, an email address, Instagram DMs, Facebook messages, a phone call. Nobody sees everything in one place.
  • Most inquiries aren't a fit. Wrong date, too small, wrong service, no budget. Reading and answering all of them takes time, so the good ones wait in the same pile as the bad ones.
  • The owner is busy doing the actual work. The best time to reply is during service, on a job site or in the middle of an event, which is exactly when you can't.

A simple system that fixes most of it

You don't need a full CRM to get this right. You need four pieces:

1. One front door

Send every inquiry through one form that asks the questions you always end up asking anyway: date, group size or project size, budget range, what they're looking for. Link that form from your website, your Google Business Profile, your Instagram bio and your email signature. When people DM you, send them the form.

2. Your rules, written down

Decide what a good fit looks like. Minimum group size, dates you're open, budget floor, services you offer. Once it's written down, a system can check every inquiry against it in seconds.

3. Instant alerts for the good ones

Good fits should reach you immediately, by email or text, with everything you need to reply in one message. Everything else can wait for a scheduled review twice a day. You stop digging through a pile and start answering the right people first.

4. A fast first reply for everyone

Even inquiries that aren't a fit deserve a quick, polite answer. A short template for "not available that date" or "that's outside what we do" keeps your reputation intact and takes seconds to send.

Where automation helps

Everything above can be done by hand with a form and a spreadsheet. Automation makes it reliable. The inquiry system I build for clients does it like this:

  • Every inquiry comes in through one Google Form and lands in a Google Sheet you own.
  • It's checked against your rules automatically.
  • AI reads the free-text message for anything the rules miss, like a date written in the notes instead of the date field. It can only send an inquiry to your review pile, never approve one on its own.
  • Strong matches email you the moment they arrive. Everything else waits for a twice-daily review, and you get a summary every Monday.
  • It checks itself every morning with a test inquiry, so you know it's working.

In testing, it sorted 30 of 30 test inquiries correctly. More importantly, the owner stops being the bottleneck for figuring out which messages matter.

A quick self-check

Submit an inquiry to your own business tonight, the way a customer would. Then note how long it takes for anyone to notice it, and whether the reply you'd send would actually answer the question. If the answer is "tomorrow afternoon" or "it depends who sees it," you're probably losing jobs you never knew about.
