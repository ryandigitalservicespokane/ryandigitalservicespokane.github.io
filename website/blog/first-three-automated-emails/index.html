The 3 automated emails every local business should set up first

By Ryan Smith · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

Most local businesses think of email marketing as "the newsletter," which means it happens when someone has time, which means it mostly doesn't. The emails that actually do the heavy lifting are the automated ones: set up once, sent every time someone does something specific.

In my day job running email for a national e-commerce brand, the automated flows are where I spend a lot of my time, because they keep working without anyone hitting send. Local businesses get the same benefit at a smaller scale. If you only set up three, make it these.

1. The welcome email

When it goes out: right after someone joins your list, whether that's a signup form on your site, a QR code at the counter or a checkbox at checkout.

What it should do: thank them, tell them what to expect (how often you'll email and what about) and give them one reason to come back soon. That can be an offer, but it doesn't have to be. A "here's what people order first" or "here's what's coming up this month" works too.

Common mistake: having a signup box with no welcome at all. Someone just raised their hand, and the moment they're most interested is the moment you say nothing.

Also worth checking: that your signup form actually saves anyone. Website builders sometimes show a newsletter box that isn't connected to anything, and nobody notices for months. Submit your own form and make sure the address shows up in your email platform.

2. The review request

When it goes out: a day or two after a visit, appointment, delivery or completed job, while the experience is fresh.

What it should do: thank them by name, ask one simple question, and make leaving a review a single tap with a direct link to your Google review page.

Common mistake: asking for a review in the same email as a promotion. Keep the ask on its own so it's easy to say yes to.

Reviews feed directly into how you show up in local search and on Google Maps, so this one quietly helps your marketing everywhere else too.

3. The win-back email

When it goes out: when a customer hasn't visited or ordered in a while. What "a while" means depends on your business: maybe 60 days for a coffee shop, six months for a salon, a year for a contractor.

What it should do: remind them you exist, mention something new since they last came in, and give them an easy reason to come back.

Common mistake: leading with a discount every time. Discounts work, but if every win-back is 20% off, you train customers to wait for it. Try "here's what's new" first and save the offer for a second email.

What you need to make this work

  • A way to collect emails. A form on your website, a signup at checkout or a QR code at the counter.
  • An email platform. Klaviyo, Mailchimp or whatever your POS or booking system has built in. Many booking and POS tools can trigger emails after a visit.
  • Visit or purchase data connected to the platform, so it knows when someone last came in. This is often the trickiest part and where a little automation work pays off.

After these three

Once these are running, the next ones I'd add are an abandoned cart or abandoned booking email (if you sell or book online), a birthday email (if you collect birthdays) and a post-purchase "here's how to get the most out of it" email. But start with the three above. They're simple, they run on their own, and they keep working long after you set them up.
